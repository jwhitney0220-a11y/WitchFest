func startTurn(_ state: inout TurnState, player: inout PlayerState) {
state.baseWitchfire = witchfireForRound(state.round) // 2,3,4,5,6,7,7
state.cardsPlayedThisTurn = 0
state.availableWitchfire = state.baseWitchfire + (state.isPatient ? state.bankedWitchfire : 0)
let drawCount = state.isPatient ? 2 : 1
player.drawCards(drawCount)
}
func playCard(_ card: CardInstance, state: inout TurnState) throws {
guard card.cost <= state.availableWitchfire else { throw PlayError.notEnoughWitchfire }
guard board.hasLegalPlacement(card) else { throw PlayError.noLegalPlacement }
state.availableWitchfire -= card.cost
state.cardsPlayedThisTurn += 1
board.place(card)
}
func endTurn(_ state: inout TurnState) {
if state.cardsPlayedThisTurn == 0 && !state.isPatient {
state.patienceCountdown = max(0, state.patienceCountdown - 1)
if state.patienceCountdown == 0 { state.isPatient = true }
}
state.bankedWitchfire = state.isPatient ? state.availableWitchfire : 0
}

3.2 Final Score Formula
LaneScore(lane, player) = sum(card.remainingHealth + card.remainingWard + card.currentPower)
LaneControlBonus = 250 per standard controlled lane + 350 for center/awakened controlled lane
SurvivingBoardValue = sum(all surviving health + ward + power)
RitualPressure = floor(totalDamageDealt * 0.25)
WitnessMarks = sum(triggeredWitnessMarkValues)
SealInfluence = LaneControlBonus + SurvivingBoardValue + RitualPressure + WitnessMarks

3.3 Overtime Rule
At the end of Round 6, compare Seal Influence totals. If the scores are within 5% of each other, play one OT
round using 7 Witchfire. Final Seal Influence after OT decides the winner. There is no second OT.
let high = max(playerSealInfluence, opponentSealInfluence)
let margin = abs(playerSealInfluence - opponentSealInfluence) / max(high, 1)
if margin <= 0.05 { enterOneOvertimeRound() } else { highestSealInfluenceWins() }

4.3 Card Order Within a Lane
• On Reveal: newest card first, because the newly played card is the dramatic focal point of the turn.
• Persistent effects: oldest card first, because board setup should matter.
• Awakened/Tier 3 effects: resolve last within the lane unless card text explicitly overrides.
• Death cleanup: destroyed cards remain visually present until the current action finishes, then exit cleanly.
• Action queue: every effect creates a CombatAction with source, target, value, text label, VFX key, and score
delta.

8. Tutorial - Required First-Time Match Onboarding
The tutorial must run before the player plays their first real match. It uses a controlled random legal 20-card
tutorial deck. It must teach all basic mechanics, including Witchfire, multi-card plays, draw cadence, Patience,
Ritual Wheel resolution, targeting, keywords, scoring, hooks, deck identity, and overtime.

8.1 Tutorial Deck Requirements
• 20 cards total.
• Exactly 1 random Tier 3 from the four Season 1 anchors.
• 6 Tier 2 cards and 13 Tier 1 cards.
• At least 2 cards from each family.
• At least 1 Guard/Ward card, 1 direct damage card, 1 chain/AoE card, 1 heal/cleanse card, and 1 buff/debuff
card.
• May include hook-eligible cards, but must teach that hooks are earned from packs/boss rewards or unlocked
by currency.

WITCHFEST DESTINY: SALEM ARCANA
Complete Game Lore — Seasons 1 through 40

THE WORLD BIBLE
The World
1692 Salem, Massachusetts. The veil between worlds is thin here—thinner than anywhere else in the colonies. Magic is real. It
has always been real. But it is persecuted, driven underground by church and crown alike. The witch trials that gripped Salem
were not mass hysteria. They were a cover for something deeper. Something the Puritan authorities discovered and could not
explain, so they burned everyone who knew about it.
Beneath the American continent sleeps an entity of immense, geological scale. It is not evil. It is not good. It simply IS. It has
slept for millennia, and the seals that keep it dormant are weakening. The first seal lies beneath Salem. When it breaks, the
Witch Revolutionary War begins.
The war will cross the entire continent over forty seasons. Every river crossed, every mountain climbed, every seal broken
brings the entity closer to waking. And when it wakes, the world will have to decide: is this a monster to be destroyed, or a
being to be understood?

The Spark
The inciting event that ignites the war is called simply "The Spark." On a winter night in 1692, in a hidden chamber beneath
the Salem meeting house, a coven of Dark Magic practitioners attempts a ritual to strengthen the seal—not break it. They
believe the seal is failing and that they can reinforce it with a blood pact.
They are wrong.
The ritual backfires catastrophically. Instead of strengthening the seal, it cracks it. A surge of raw magical energy erupts from
the earth beneath Salem. Every practitioner in the chamber is either killed or transformed. The crack in the seal releases
something: not the entity itself, but a pulse of its dreaming consciousness. Every magic user in Salem feels it. Every card in
existence flickers. The awakened powers—the four Tier 3 signature cards—manifest for the first time.
A young cooper's apprentice, working late in a shop near the meeting house, sees the light erupt from the ground. This
apprentice—later known only as The Witness—writes the first entry in what will become a journal spanning ten years and forty
seasons of war:
"I saw the earth open tonight. Not a crack in the ground, but a crack in what I thought was real. The light that came out was
not fire. It was older than fire. It looked at me. I think it looked at everyone. And I think it was asking a question I don't know
how to answer."
The Puritan authorities blame the coven. Arrests begin. But the magic is already spreading. The crack in the seal awakened
dormant powers in dozens of people across Salem—people who didn't know they had magic, people who had hidden it, people
who had inherited it from ancestors burned at the stake. Within days, the factions form. Within weeks, the first battles are
fought. Within a season, Salem is burning.
And watching from the shadows, a figure in a dark cloak observes everything. A silver clasp at their throat catches the
firelight. The Jailer has been waiting for this moment for three hundred years.

Corvus. They practice curses, drain, sacrifice, and corruption. They
were performing the ritual that triggered the Spark—but were they
trying to save the seal, or were they manipulated into breaking it?

The Big Mystery
Why does magic exist in this world? What is the veil? Who or what benefits from the war continuing indefinitely?
The answer sleeps beneath America.
The entity is not a monster. It is not a god. It is a being of such vast scale and slow consciousness that its dreams take
centuries to complete. The seals were not a cage built by humans to imprison it. The seals were a bargain—an agreement
made thousands of years ago between the entity and the first peoples of this land. The entity chose to sleep. The seals were the
terms of that sleep.
But something changed. Someone began breaking the seals. And that someone—the Jailer—is not the villain of this story. The
Jailer was the first explorer to discover the entity, centuries ago. Their life was bound to the seals in exchange for forbidden
knowledge. For three hundred years, they maintained the seals. But now they are breaking them, one by one, working
westward.

The war is not about winning. It is about choosing what kind of world the entity wakes into.

The Witness
An ordinary Salem Folk who saw the Spark happen. A cooper's apprentice, age 22 when the story begins. They were working

Season 6: The Witness dies. Killed during a battle in the Delta. And then—three days later—they return. Changed. Their skin is

Season 9: The Witness, now a middle-aged veteran of a decade-long war, has become a faction leader in their own right. They
lead not with power but with knowledge—they have seen more of the entity than anyone alive.
Season 10: The Witness names the entity. Naming gives it form, and form gives it limits. This is the final choice of the war—not
a battle, but a naming. And the Witness, who started as a terrified apprentice with a journal, is the only one who can do it.

SEASON 1: SALEM ARCANA
Detailed Narrative
ACT 1: THE SPARK IGNITES
Opening Cutscene: The Spark Event Duration: 90 seconds. The Witness narrates throughout.
It is winter in Salem, 1692. Snow on the rooftops. Lanterns swinging in the wind. The meeting house stands dark and
silent—but beneath it, in a chamber carved from the earth, a coven works in secret. Mother Corvus leads the ritual, her silver
athame raised. Around her, Dark Magic practitioners chant in a language older than English, older than Latin. They are trying
to strengthen the seal. They believe they are saving the world.
The ritual circle is drawn in ash and blood. Candles burn at seven points. The air is thick with incense and anticipation.
The Witness, a young cooper's apprentice, is working late three streets away. They hear nothing. See nothing. But they feel
something—a pressure change, like before a storm. They look up from their work.
In the chamber, the ritual reaches its climax. Mother Corvus speaks the final word.
And the seal cracks.
Not a physical crack. A crack in reality itself. Violet light erupts from the center of the ritual circle. The candles extinguish
simultaneously. The ash and blood ignite. The practitioners are thrown backward. Three of them are killed instantly. Two are
transformed—their bodies becoming conduits for the escaping energy, their eyes burning with violet fire.
Mother Corvus alone remains standing. She has seen this coming. This was not the ritual's intended outcome—but it was one
she had prepared for. As the light pours out of the earth, she smiles.
Above ground, the Witness sees the light. It erupts from behind the meeting house like a second sunrise—but violet, not gold.

The Pacific Veilkeepers—the guardians of the final seal—are introduced. They are the last line: an alliance of Chinese railroad
workers, Hawaiian priests, Russian fur traders, and the indigenous peoples of the coast. They do not seek to win the war. They
seek to ensure that when the entity rises, it rises on their terms.

SEASON 10: THE NAMING OF THE LAND (Summary)
Region: West Coast, the Final Seal Key Events:
The entity rises. Not in a single moment of apocalyptic rupture, but slowly. The ground swells. The sky bends. The ocean at the
edge of the continent begins to glow. The entity is not a creature that bursts from the earth. It IS the earth. It has always been
the earth. The continent is its body. The seals were the lullaby that kept it sleeping.
The final confrontation is not a battle. It is a choice, presented to the player and to the Witness. The entity is awake. It is
disoriented. Its dreams—ten thousand years of accumulated imagery, memory, emotion—are spilling into the physical world. If
it is not given form, it will remain formless, and its formlessness will reshape the continent in chaotic, unpredictable ways.
Someone must name it. Naming gives form, and form gives limits. The Witness, who started as a terrified apprentice with a
journal, is the only one who can do it. They know the entity. They have carried its voice. They have died and returned with its

Ending 2: The Wrathful Awakening. The Witness names the entity with fear and accusation. The entity wakes angry—centuries
of dreams twisted by the violence of the war, by the fragments that escaped, by the careless breaking of seals. It is not evil, but
it is hurt. And a hurt continent is a dangerous thing. The factions must now manage a being of geological scale that does not

Ending 3: The Witness's Sacrifice. The Witness does not name the entity. Instead, they merge with it—offering their own
consciousness as the stabilizing form. The Witness ceases to exist as an individual. They become the entity's voice. The entity
wakes, and when it speaks, it speaks with the Witness's voice, uses the Witness's words, remembers everything the Witness
wrote. The journal becomes the entity's first memory of the surface world.
Ending 4: The Jailer's Redemption. If the player supported the Jailer throughout, the Jailer offers their own name as the final
seal. They have been bound to the seals for three centuries. They can become the final binding—not to keep the entity asleep,
but to give it a stable anchor in the waking world. The Jailer merges with the final seal. The entity wakes. The Jailer is
gone—but their silver clasp remains, embedded in the earth where the final seal was, catching the sunrise.
Final Image: The North American continent, seen from above. The entity's form—whatever it has become—is visible as a
pattern of light beneath the land. The war is over. What comes next is not a battle. It is a relationship. The continent is alive.
